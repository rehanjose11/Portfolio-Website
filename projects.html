IN PROGRESS

NextGen mParivahan

I started this project as part of my Interface Design course this semester after noticing something that made mParivahan different from a typical app: for many Indians, there isn’t really an alternative. When a digital service is this essential, I felt simply redesigning the interface wasn’t enough—I wanted to understand what was actually making the experience difficult and try to solve those problems through design.

The project has now grown into a deeper UX investigation, looking at the gap between mParivahan’s massive reach and user satisfaction through secondary research, app-store reviews, and interface evaluation. I’m currently moving into primary research and usability testing to validate the problems before defining the redesign.

If you find this project interesting or have thoughts, insights, or experience that could help shape the research, feel free to reach out—I’d love to hear from you.

IN PROGRESS

Accessibility in Exams

I started this project with a simple question: Indian competitive examinations provide provisions for candidates with disabilities—but how accessible is the process of applying for them?

As part of my Inclusive Design course, I’m exploring the gap between accessibility provisions and the actual application experience. While accommodations such as scribes, extra time, and other considerations may be available at the examination stage, the digital journey to access these provisions can involve its own barriers. I’m currently researching how candidates discover information, understand eligibility, complete applications, and request accommodations, with the aim of identifying where the application experience falls short of being truly inclusive.

This is an ongoing research project, and I’m currently working through the research and evaluation stage before moving towards potential design interventions.

If you have experience navigating competitive examination applications, accessibility provisions, or inclusive digital services, feel free to reach out. Your perspective could help shape this research.
