Chapter 01

Understanding Xplor AI Mobility

Founded in late 2023 at the Kerala Technology Innovation Zone in Kochi, Xplor AI Mobility is an Intelligent Mobility as a Service (iMaaS) company building a connected digital ecosystem for public transportation and fleet management.

The Real-World Crisis

In India, millions rely on private buses and public transit daily. However, operations are plagued by uncoordinated schedules, manual paper tickets, zero live arrival tracking, and complete disconnect between metro and feeder services.

AI-Powered iMaaS

Xplor transforms fragmented transit by connecting operators, vehicles, drivers, and commuters through a centralized software platform—integrating ticketing, automated scheduling, GPS telemetry, and passenger discovery.

Multimodal Vision

From Kochi Metro feeder bus tie-ups to Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) mobility protocols, Xplor unites isolated transit modes into a single, seamless commuter journey.

Xplor User App

A commuter-facing application enabling users to search routes, check real-time bus arrivals, buy metro/bus digital tickets, and plan multimodal journeys across the city.

Fleet Operations Platform

Tools for bus owners and transit authorities to monitor fleets in real-time, audit ticketing revenue, manage crew schedules, and improve route efficiency.

Kochi Metro Feeder Network

Digital scheduling and first/last-mile integration connecting Kochi Metro stations with feeder buses, reducing commuter transfer wait times.

Halo by Xplor

A dedicated school and institutional transportation ERP providing parent ETA alerts, route optimization, driver monitoring, and child safety attendance.

Chapter 03

The 42-App Benchmarking Study

To transition Xplor from a single-point ticketing tool into a comprehensive mobility companion, I conducted an in-depth competitive benchmarking analysis evaluating 42 international and domestic mobility and ERP platforms.

Global Transit Benchmarks

Analyzed Citymapper, Moovit, Transit, Whim, and Chalo. Studied how leading platforms present multimodal transfers, walking radii, live vehicle tracking, and real-time disruption alerts.

Ride-Hailing & Intercity

Evaluated Uber, Rapido, Namma Yatri, RedBus, and ixigo to extract best-in-class location picking patterns, payment flows, fare transparency, and post-booking reassurance.

Institutional ERP Systems

Analyzed school transit software to benchmark feature sets for Halo by Xplor—including guardian geo-fencing, student attendance check-ins, and emergency protocol communications.

"The central takeaway from our benchmarking: Modern public transit users do not just want to buy a ticket. They want certainty—knowing exactly when a bus will arrive, where to switch to the metro, and how to reach their final destination with minimal friction."
Chapter 04

Systemic UX & Interaction Frameworks

Public transit commuters often use mobile applications while on moving buses, in crowded stations, and with intermittent cellular connectivity. Good UX in this space demands reducing cognitive load and providing absolute clarity at every step.

Search & Location Swap Logic

In the original app, selecting a boarding and destination point auto-navigated to results. However, when commuters returned to swap or change their route, the lack of an explicit confirmation action created user doubt. I introduced contextual behavior: Instant auto-advance for initial searches, and an explicit "Proceed" confirmation CTA during route modifications.

Availability States & Demand Capture

When a direct bus was unavailable between two stops, the existing experience left commuters at a dead end. I framed three distinct state flows: Direct Available, Indirect / Feeder Transfer Available, and No Service Yet—pairing the dead-end state with an intentional demand-capture button to log unserved routes for future operator planning.

Push Notification Architecture

Crafted a comprehensive push notification taxonomy categorized by urgency: Critical Journey Alerts (e.g. bus approaching stop in 2 mins), Operational Disruption Notices, and Value-Driven Updates—establishing concise, regional-friendly UX microcopy that respects commuter attention.

End-to-End Cross-Platform Auditing

Conducted systematic QA usability passes across different screen resolutions and OS builds. Documented visual inconsistencies, touch-target issues on budget Android devices, and state handling errors, bridging the gap between design vision and technical execution.

Project 04 Marketing Strategy & Visual Communication

Bus Ticket Back-of-Ticket Advertising System

Format: 8 × 5 cm Thermal Paper (CorelDraw CDR)
Duration: 08 June – 17 June 2026 (10 Days)
Daily Scale: 200+ Buses · 150,000+ Tickets Issued Daily
Reach: 50,000+ Commuters · 3–5 Min Attention Dwell

Xplor identified an underutilized physical asset in private transit operations: the blank reverse side of thermal paper tickets printed across 200+ partner buses in 11 districts of Kerala. With conductors issuing ~750 tickets per bus daily, this touchpoint generates 150,000+ daily physical impressions with an average commuter dwell time of 3 to 5 minutes during transit rides.

I engineered a dual-stream advertising system tailored to the strict constraints of high-speed handheld thermal ticket printers—demanding high contrast, zero dependency on color gradients, and legible typography at small print dimensions.
